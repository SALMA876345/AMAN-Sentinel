Decide in this order

You are judging whether this evidence settles the claim, not whether the claim is true. Stop at the first step that answers it.

  1. Anything to read? One word or a fragment settles nothing. Unverified
  2. Independent? If it only repeats the claim, copied or as other posts spreading the same story, it is the claim circulating. Unverified
  3. Same event? Same place or person but a different event does not count. Unverified
  4. Settled? A report or statement confirms it Real, or denies or debunks it Fake. Discussed but not settled Unverified

Repetition is never confirmation. Steps 1 to 3 can only give Unverified.

1 · Verdict

Apply the four steps above.

2 · Evidence relevance

How close does it come to the specific assertion?

3 · Faithfulness

Does this evidence, on its own, carry a definite verdict? High only if your verdict is Real or Fake and a reader could reach it from this evidence alone. Unverified is always Low.

4 · Confidence

Could you defend a different verdict? Unsure is not a penalty; it tells adjudication where to look.

Describes the event and adds a detail (some losses), but reads like viral headlines rather than a named report. Either Real or Unverified is defensible. Pick one and mark Unsure.

Relevance and faithfulness scales
Relevant
Addresses the specific claim, its entities and its time frame.
Partially relevant
Same topic or entity, not the specific assertion.
Irrelevant
Different topic, different event, or generic boilerplate.
Faithfulness High
Your verdict is Real or Fake, and this evidence alone is enough to reach it.
Faithfulness Low
Your verdict leans on outside knowledge or thin evidence, or your verdict is Unverified.

Do not look claims up. Knowing a claim is false does not make this evidence refute it.
